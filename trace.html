Technical Walkthrough

Capturing an uncooperative or compliant satellite handle from a free-floating chaser is difficult because every arm movement pushes back on the chaser's base, while the gripper physically blocks the camera view right before contact. I built this MuJoCo simulation to test closed-loop visual capture between two robotic systems:

  • GRALS: A rail-mounted manipulator holding a satellite mock-up and capture handle, configurable as either rigid or dynamically compliant.
  • ORBIT: A 6-DoF UR5e arm mounted on an unactuated planar floating base (\(x, y, \psi\)), equipped with a parallel gripper and an onboard camera.

Ground-truth poses are isolated strictly for post-run evaluation; the vision tracker and arm controller rely only on rendered onboard images and measured robot states.

1. System & Capture Pipeline

Physics steps at 500 Hz while the onboard vision loop updates at 30 Hz. During a capture run, observations and commands flow through five stages:

Onboard Camera Rendering (30 Hz, 480×360)
ArUco Detection & Handle Pose/Velocity Filter
Camera-to-World Pre-Grasp Target Transform
Free-Floating Generalized-Jacobian Control (500 Hz)
Capture State Machine & Contact / Slip Monitor

2. Onboard Vision & Occlusion Handling

At 30 Hz, the tracker detects the \(0.112\,\mathrm{m}\) ArUco marker mounted on the satellite mock-up and estimates its 6-DoF pose via Perspective-n-Point (PnP). It filters handle position and velocity in the OpenCV camera frame (\(+X\) right, \(+Y\) down, \(+Z\) forward) and maps the estimate into world coordinates using ORBIT's measured camera pose:

\[T_{W,H} = T_{W,\mathrm{cam}}\, T_{\mathrm{cam},M}\, T_{M,H}\]

Because the onboard camera looks straight down the wrist, the closing gripper fingers inevitably block the marker during terminal grasp. If marker detections drop during free-space reach, tracking flags as stale and the arm holds position safely. Once a validated close sequence begins, the controller switches to regulating the last accepted handle pose at reduced gain until opposing finger contact is confirmed.

3. Free-Floating Resolved-Rate Control

Because ORBIT's planar base has no thrusters or wheel actuators, accelerating the arm shifts and rotates the base in the opposite direction. A fixed-base Jacobian ignores this recoil and causes the gripper to miss the handle. Instead, I reduce the arm and base Jacobians (\(J_m, J_b\)) using the coupled base–arm inertia submatrices (\(H_b, H_{bm}\)) to form a generalized Jacobian \(J^*\):

\[J^* = J_m - J_b H_b^{-1} H_{bm}, \qquad \dot{q} = J^{*T}\left(J^* J^{*T} + \lambda^2 I\right)^{-1} e\]

Solving damped least squares with \(J^*\) accounts for base reaction during free motion so the gripper tracks the visual target cleanly even as the passive base drifts.

4. Capture Sequence & Target Compliance

The capture supervisor advances through five states: Track → Pre-Grasp → Approach → Close → Hold. Once the \(76\,\mathrm{mm}\) gripper aligns over the handle, the fingers close and a contact monitor checks for opposing pad forces and post-grasp tangential slip.

To evaluate capture dynamics under realistic contact transients, GRALS can hold the satellite rigidly or yield through a bounded 6D admittance model driven by contact wrench \(F_{\mathrm{ext}}\):

\[M_a \ddot{x}_c + D_a \dot{x}_c + K_a x_c = F_{\mathrm{ext}}\]

In compliant mode, the target yields smoothly when touched by the gripper, reducing peak impact forces and preventing jamming if minor visual estimation errors remain at contact.

6. Parameters & Validation

Every run logs ground-truth separated evaluation summaries covering vision pose error, pre-grasp reach accuracy, opposing finger contact forces, slip, and passive-base drift, alongside an 8-second headless numerical stability and base-reaction verification check.
